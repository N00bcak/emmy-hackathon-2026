# Test relevance and tolerance audit

**Implemented on `n00bcak`:** the audited 65-case core is now the repository
default in `tests/gpu-regression.json`. See [active gate identities](active-gates.json)
and [usage](../gpu-regression.md). The original audit and its inactive proposal
below describe the decision before activation; historical fixtures and policy
remain intact. GPU qualification still requires a new suite identity.

The compiler acceptance rule is final-output error within a justified case tolerance, with correct output membership, ABI, bounds, synchronization and state. Selective FTZ, approximate math, FMA, vectorization and reduction reassociation are permitted when they meet that rule. No test earns a universal optimization veto merely by being in the archive.

## Scope and evidence

Audited every one of the 100 bundled cases individually, the archived per-lane witness, and the two historical exp-rescaling witnesses. Typed IR was deserialized with Emmy; actual input/reference arrays, operation kinds, dimensions and tolerances were inspected for the first 101 cases. The two historical exp cases were audited from their source-bound receipts. No compiler, fixture, tolerance or active gate was changed; no new case was generated. GPU results cited here are the existing main0914413 comparison, not new measurements.

All 169 checked-in Python test methods are inventoried. The 32 compiler test methods have assertion-level assessments; other methods are classified by tool ownership and policy role, not claimed as a complete proof of their internal logic.

## Proposed routine gate

Bundled100 dispositions: mandatory: 15, targeted: 28, core: 50, diagnostic: 6, replace: 1. The proposed routine core contains 65 cases including the required 15. Targeted tests still earn their keep when the corresponding mechanism/dtype changes; they are not deleted. Diagnostic tests retain failures without turning adversarial conditioning into a blanket precision contract. The proposed profile is inactive; the original 93-case policy and 100-case campaign suite retain their identities.

## Relevant bug types

- Wrong ABI/order, scalar-vs-pointer arguments, intermediate buffer ownership or pipeline order.
- Wrong output membership, indices, tails, vector alignment, memory widths or buffer contents.
- Incorrect active-lane identities, collective participation, shared-memory visibility, async stage lifetime or atomic reset.
- Stale expression/address caching, scope leaks, lost seeds and mistaken logical/hardware thread identity.
- Wrong integer operations or numeric/bitcast conversions, restricted to supported dtype/operation paths.
- Wrong mathematical function or normalization graph, unsafe value reuse between different queries, or approximation that exceeds final-output tolerance.
- Range/fallback failures are relevant to the range promised by a fast path; they do not imply all ordinary inputs need the slow path.

## Actual obstructions found

1. Serial shared-dot cases 70/73 combine 2^30 cancellation with zero tolerance. They freeze serial rounding and can reject reasonable FMA/reassociation. Keep them as diagnostics; preserve their seed/tail/alignment boundaries in a separately validated bounded-input oracle before routine gating.
2. Attention cases 77/78 reach 1e10 in q inputs and deliberately expose sequential-rounding sensitivity. Their full/tail geometry matters; their adversarial conditioning is not a universal ordinary-workload gate.
3. The archived per-lane witness catches a genuine membership bug, but also has a zero-tolerance serial cancellation oracle. Fix the ownership eligibility test; do not infer that every reassociation is illegal. A mathematical example with x[i]=i already distinguishes changed output membership 192 versus 6 without delicate rounding. No such new fixture was generated in this audit.
4. Standalone contraction 92 has all-zero expected outputs. It can detect some spurious contributions, but cannot certify ordinary nonzero accumulation or a replacement matmul fast path.
5. The max-finite TF32 witness 99 is useful range/fallback stress and the only MMA-active case in the 100. Treating it as grounds to disable TF32 globally is an implementation choice, not a test requirement. Conversely, simply dropping it leaves no ordinary MMA numerical coverage.
6. The selector and two policy-lock CPU tests mandate retention of starred operators, despite their exclusion from the stated challenge. That is a versioned extra-support policy, not a challenge rule. Keep the old policy immutable and state a new profile explicitly if it is activated.
7. Backend CPU tests require particular moves, labels, load counts, native division instructions, loop backedges and even calculations whose results are dead. Valid copy elimination, DCE, strength reduction, predication or unrolling can fail those assertions. Their semantic scenarios matter; their instruction spelling should be a targeted mechanism check, not a universal correctness condition.

No pair was declared redundant solely because the names/family match. Width 8 versus 16, width 127 versus 257, lead 2 versus 3 and extent below/equal to prefill lead exercise different branches. Their individual ledger entries state the distinction.

## FTZ policy

Current compile.py uses ex2.approx.ftz.f32 on the usual path and, for scaled arguments below -125, shifts by 126 then multiplies by 2^-126 without FTZ to preserve tiny results. This is already approximate, not exact arithmetic.

The two historical exp-rescaling witnesses earn generic-exp/range coverage: flushing a tiny value and later multiplying it by a huge factor can violate a nonzero output tolerance. They do not prove ordinary normalized softmax needs the same policy. A recognized normalized softmax has a shifted denominator at least 1; dropping subnormal exponentials has an ordinary finite error bound proportional to the number of terms. Any later multiplication/reuse must be included in the final error argument. A threshold chosen for generic exp is not a universal downstream-error certificate.

## Precision assessment

Zero tolerance alone is not grounds to remove a test. Exact I64 shifts, raw copies, indices and small exact-integer scope tests catch semantic corruption without constraining approximate exp. Bounded seeded shared-dot cases 71/72 use small integer inputs and expected values at most 36: reassociation/FMA does not need to alter those results. Actual values, not just test labels, control the disposition.

No fixture tolerance was loosened. A replacement oracle must use independent mathematical references and a stated error budget under a new evaluation version; both comparison arms then need source-bound requalification.
